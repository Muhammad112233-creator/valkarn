The world of Valkarn

Nine gates were raised at the edge of the known map, and nobody living remembers who raised them. Valkarn has walked through eight. This is the world on the other side of them.

Why read Valkarn?

The gods set a man against his own fate

A foundling of unknown blood is pulled out of the surf below Haldstad and raised, grudgingly, by the Northfolk. He becomes the best archer on the coast. He marries the jarl's daughter, which is treason. Everything after that is consequence.

What keeps readers here for seventy-four volumes is not the swordplay. It is that the saga treats freedom as an expensive thing: every time Valkarn buys a quiet year for his family, somebody sends an invoice.

Where to start

Three honest entry points

Volume one if you like being thrown in. The Hollow Fjord if you want the saga at full strength. Farewell to the Shieldmaiden if you would rather read one complete book before committing to forty-two.

Read volume one

A world that does not forgive

The north of this saga is not decorative. Winters kill, debts are inherited, and the gods are less interested in worship than in leverage.

Underneath the sagas sits an older story: a drowned people who left their world and never quite arrived in this one. The saga takes its time telling you that, and it is better for the wait.
